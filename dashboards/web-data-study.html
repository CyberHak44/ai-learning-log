STEP 1 OF 5 · WHAT WEB DATA COLLECTION IS

A script reads public pages the way you would, thousands of times

When you search Google or open a website, your browser asks a server for a page and shows it. A scraper does the same with a script: it asks for many pages and pulls out the useful parts, such as a domain name, a price or a company name, into a table. It only sees what any visitor could see.

Think of it as

A researcher reading every newspaper each morning and noting each mention of your company. The script is a team of a thousand researchers who never tire.

Say it in one line

"Web data collection automates reading public pages and turns them into structured data; it sees only what any visitor can see."

STEP 2 OF 5 · WHY PROXIES

Sites block one address asking too often

If thousands of requests come from one internet address, websites slow them down or block them. A proxy sends each request from a different address. Different proxy types look like different kinds of visitor, and cost differently.

Think of it as

Posting letters. Send a thousand from one post office and the sorting office gets suspicious. Spread them across many post offices and each looks normal.

STEP 3 OF 5 · APIs DO THE HARD PART

Pay per result, not per headache

Running your own proxies means handling blocks, CAPTCHAs and broken pages. Oxylabs' APIs do that for you: you send one request saying what you want, and you get clean data back. /Search returns search engine results. /Scrape returns a page's content, optionally already turned into structured data. You pay per thousand results, and failed requests from their side are not billed.

STEP 4 OF 5 · HOW BRAND WATCH WORKS

From a client's name to a monthly report

Fake invoice fraud usually starts with a look-alike: a domain one letter off the client's, or a copied website. Brand Watch looks for them every month, so the client hears it from IE before a supplier gets a fake invoice.

Say it in one line

"Brand Watch searches for look-alikes of the client's name each month, reads the suspect pages, scores them and reports what to take down."

STEP 5 OF 5 · THE LEGAL LINE

Public business data yes. People's data and break-ins no.

The UAE personal data law (PDPL) has no "legitimate interests" basis like the UK GDPR, so collecting personal data needs consent, a contract or another listed basis. Oxylabs' own terms also ban non-public data, logging in without permission and any security testing through its network. Stay on the green side.
